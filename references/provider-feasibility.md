# Prove the public audience before proposing a provider-backed PWA

When a product depends on an external account, the first feasibility question is whether the stated audience can actually finish signup and receive the core data. Check the provider's current official registration mode, per-app authorized-user limit, review requirements, rate/quota constraints, and which endpoint returns the needed data. Count completed functional accounts, not login-page visits. If the user's intended launch is public, quantify its minimum reach before recommending a pilot size; do not silently redefine a launch as a small invitation-only test.

For each viable-looking alternative, describe the actual visitor path and which requirement it weakens: another account, a platform community, an extension, a native helper, self-reporting, weaker timeliness, or permission to share activity. Distinguish a documented right to connect 100 accounts from observed quality at 100. Before ranking, name the objective and the strongest other route, then perform the smallest safe check that could overturn the ranking.

Incident, 2026-09-24, Same Song: Tejas asked for a PWA to connect people listening to the same song, intending to post it publicly as a reply to a tweet. The first survey proposed a five-person Spotify developer-mode pilot, even though Spotify's published developer limit meant it could not serve the implied audience. Tejas corrected it: "Right now, this is not feasible. This is definitely not feasible here. I mean, I'm gonna post it on Twitter as a reply, I mean, it has the potential to go viral. I mean, we need to support at least 100 people at the minimum. After that, it could break down and that's the, I don't care. But like, what do you mean, 5 people? 5 people is like no nothing, really." The mistaken first theory was that proving the interaction with five users was an adequate product recommendation. The corrected rule is to establish the audience floor and provider admission contract first. The resulting route survey lives in the Same Song project, `docs/100-person-survey.md`.
